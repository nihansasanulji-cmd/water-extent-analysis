About the Project

1. The Problem

Labugama and Kalatuwawa are reservoirs that feed water supply for the Colombo region. Changes in their surface water extent affect supply security, yet they are rarely tracked in a simple, visual, and repeatable way.

2. Study Area & Data

Study Area: Labugama & Kalatuwawa Reservoirs, Western Province, Sri Lanka

Data Source: Cloud-free Landsat 8/9 OLI multi-spectral imagery (2019 & 2026), courtesy of NASA/USGS.

Coordinate Reference System (CRS): WGS 1984 UTM Zone 44N (used for accurate metric area calculations).

3. Methodology

  1. Image Acquisition & Preprocessing: Selected low-cloud-cover Landsat scenes for 2019 and 2026.
  2. NDWI Spectral Calculation: Computed Normalized Difference Water Index using Band 3 (Green) and Band 5 (NIR): NDWI = (Green - NIR) / (Green + NIR).
  3. Thresholding & Delineation: Isolated open water surfaces from surrounding land and converted rasters into vector polygons.
  4. Smoothing & Dissolve: Applied PAEK (Polynomial Approximation with Exponential Kernel) smoothing and Pairwise Dissolve in ArcGIS Pro to refine reservoir boundaries.
  5. Spatial Comparison & Web GIS: Calculated exact surface areas (km²) and published layers via Leaflet.js.

4. Water Area Change & Results Interpretation

Reservoir 2019 Area 2026 Area Net Change
Kalatuwawa 1.33 km² 0.98 km² -0.35 km² (-26.17%)
Labugama 0.66 km² 0.62 km² -0.04 km² (-6.60%)

*Note: Percentage reductions are calculated directly from unrounded raw vector geometry measurements in ArcGIS Pro.

Results Interpretation: Both reservoirs experienced surface water shrinkage between 2019 and 2026. Kalatuwawa showed a significantly higher reduction (-26.17%) compared to Labugama (-6.60%), likely due to differences in basin morphometry and surrounding dense forest buffer protection at Labugama.

5. Limitations & Future Solutions

  • Limitations: 30m spatial resolution limits detection of narrow shoreline changes; seasonal rainfall variations between acquisition dates can influence water levels.
  • Solutions & Impact: Provides water managers (NWSDB) a low-cost, repeatable decision-support workflow for early drought planning, catchment protection, and multi-year monitoring.

6. Conclusion

Integrating Landsat NDWI remote sensing with interactive Web GIS effectively reveals multi-temporal reservoir depletion patterns, offering an accessible tool for sustainable urban water resource management.
