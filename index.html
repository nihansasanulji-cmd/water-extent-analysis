About the Project

1. The Problem

Labugama and Kalatuwawa are key reservoirs supplying drinking water to the Colombo region. Changes in their surface water extent directly affect water supply security, yet they are rarely tracked in a simple, visual, and repeatable way.

2. Study Area & Satellite Data

Study Area: Labugama & Kalatuwawa Reservoirs, Western Province, Sri Lanka

Data Source: Cloud-free Landsat 8/9 (Collection 2, Level-2) multi-spectral imagery downloaded via USGS EarthExplorer for January 3, 2019 (2019-01-03) and September 12, 2026 (2026-09-12).

Coordinate System (CRS): WGS 1984 UTM Zone 44N (used for accurate metric area calculations).

3. ArcGIS Pro Methodology

  1. Band Selection: Imported Band 3 (Green) and Band 5 (Near-Infrared / NIR) into ArcGIS Pro for both 2019 and 2026.
  2. NDWI Calculation: Computed the Normalized Difference Water Index using the Raster Calculator (Spatial Analyst):
    NDWI = Float(Band 3 [Green] - Band 5 [NIR]) / Float(Band 3 [Green] + Band 5 [NIR])
  3. Water Extraction (Thresholding): Extracted water pixels using the Conditional (Con) tool with a threshold greater than -0.1:
    Con("NDWI_2019.tif" > -0.1, 1)   Con("NDWI_2026.tif" > -0.1, 1)
  4. Polygon Conversion & Smoothing: Converted water rasters to vector features using Raster to Polygon and refined shorelines using the Smooth Polygon (PAEK) tool.
  5. Water Loss Overlay & Cleaning: Used the Erase tool (2019 minus 2026) to extract exposed shorelines, split features with Multipart To Singlepart, calculated area in km² via Calculate Geometry, and deleted sub-pixel slivers smaller than 0.0009 km² (one 30m × 30m Landsat pixel).
  6. Reservoir Aggregation (Dissolve): Assigned each polygon to its reservoir and used the Dissolve tool (Data Management) to sum the total water loss area (SUM_km2) separately for Kalatuwawa and Labugama.
  7. Projection & Web GIS Export: Projected all layers to GCS_WGS_1984 (EPSG:4326) using the Project tool, exported them to GeoJSON via Features To JSON, and built this interactive Leaflet.js map.

5. Spatial Analysis & Discussion

Key Analytical Insights:
  • Higher Vulnerability at Kalatuwawa: Kalatuwawa lost 0.3522 km² (26.17%) of its surface water, which is more than eight times the area lost by Labugama (0.0418 km² or 6.60%).
  • Shoreline Retreat Pattern: The red overlay layer shows that water retreat mainly occurred along shallow northern and eastern bays of Kalatuwawa, while Labugama maintained a much more stable water boundary due to steeper surrounding terrain.
  • Management Priority: Because Kalatuwawa experiences larger water-level fluctuations, it requires closer dry-season storage monitoring and stricter buffer-zone protection along exposed shorelines.

6. Proposed Solutions & Practical Impact

  • Easy & Low-Cost Monitoring: Gives water authorities (like NWSDB) a free, repeatable way to track reservoir changes using open-access Landsat imagery instead of relying solely on costly field surveys.
  • Better Dry-Season Planning: Helps water managers plan ahead for dry weather, manage distribution carefully, and reduce drinking water shortages in the Colombo area.
  • Catchment Protection: Helps identify exposed shorelines (highlighted in red) to prevent illegal land encroachment and prioritize tree-planting around reservoir catchments.

7. Limitations

  • Seasonal Weather Differences: Images were captured in different months (January 2019 vs. September 2026), so seasonal rainfall variations can influence water levels.
  • 30-Meter Resolution: Landsat's 30m pixel size limits the detection of very small or narrow shoreline shifts.
  • Two-Date Comparison & Cloud Cover: Comparing only two dates does not show year-by-year fluctuations, and optical imagery is restricted by tropical cloud cover.
