About the Project

1. The Problem

Labugama and Kalatuwawa are major drinking water reservoirs supplying the Colombo Metropolitan Region. Changes in their surface water extent directly affect water supply security, yet they are rarely tracked in a simple, visual, and repeatable way.

2. Study Area & Data Sources

  • Study Area: Labugama & Kalatuwawa Reservoirs, Western Province, Sri Lanka.
  • Satellite Data: Cloud-free Landsat 8/9 OLI multi-spectral imagery (2019 & 2026), courtesy of NASA/USGS.
  • Coordinate Reference System (CRS): WGS 1984 UTM Zone 44N (used for accurate planar area computation).

3. Geospatial Methodology

  1. NDWI Calculation: Derived surface water index in ArcGIS Pro using Green (Band 3) and NIR (Band 5): NDWI = (Green - NIR) / (Green + NIR).
  2. Delineation & Vectorization: Isolated positive NDWI water pixels via thresholding and converted raster extents into vector polygons.
  3. Geometry Refinement: Applied PAEK (Polynomial Approximation with Exponential Kernel) polygon smoothing and Pairwise Dissolve to create clean reservoir boundaries.
  4. Area Computation & Web GIS: Computed surface areas (km²) and published interactive GeoJSON layers via Leaflet.js.

4. Results & Water Area Change

Reservoir 2019 Area 2026 Area Net Area Change
Kalatuwawa 1.33 km² 0.98 km² -0.35 km² (-26.17%)
Labugama 0.66 km² 0.62 km² -0.04 km² (-6.60%)

*Note: Percentage changes are computed from exact unrounded vector geometry values in ArcGIS Pro.

Results Interpretation & Conclusion: Both reservoirs experienced surface water shrinkage between 2019 and 2026. Kalatuwawa recorded a significantly higher reduction (-26.17%) compared to Labugama (-6.60%), which is better buffered by dense surrounding forest reserve cover. This highlights the urgent need for continuous satellite-based reservoir monitoring and proactive water management.

5. Limitations & Practical Solutions

  • Limitations: 30m Landsat spatial resolution constraints, seasonal water-level fluctuations, and comparison across two discrete time periods.
  • Proposed Solutions: Provides water authorities (NWSDB) with a low-cost, repeatable decision-support dashboard for early drought risk planning, water allocation strategies, and catchment conservation.

6. How to Use the Map

Toggle the 2019 and 2026 layers, adjust the opacity slider to compare extents, use the quick-zoom buttons for each reservoir, and click directly on any reservoir polygon to view detailed statistics.
